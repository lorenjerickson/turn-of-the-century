# Campaign Prompt: The Enemy Within

Use this prompt together with the campaign generation prep prompt. Generate a campaign called `The Enemy Within` for Turn of the Century.

This prompt is for campaign generation only. Do not write a complete scenario, encounter script, room-by-room adventure, stat block set, or finished handout collection. The first scenario may be outlined because it establishes the campaign, but it must remain a campaign-level scenario seed suitable for later expansion.

## Core Premise

The campaign begins in London circa 1890, at the height of Victorian confidence, imperial ceremony, industrial expansion, social anxiety, and gothic dread. The player characters begin in custody at Scotland Yard. They have been gathered by arrest, suspicion, mistaken association, political convenience, or some other pressure suitable to their backgrounds, and their release depends upon assisting with a matter the authorities cannot acknowledge publicly.

Their first scenario should establish the campaign's world view: London as a great engine of civilisation whose mechanisms conceal poverty, corruption, scientific hubris, spiritual hunger, and institutional fear. It should include investigative play, gothic horror, social pressure, dangerous pursuit, and at least one encounter that demonstrates that the unnatural in this setting has material roots rather than fantasy magic.

The long campaign should carry the party from London to places of gothic, scientific, archaeological, industrial, and colonial interest around the world. Each major location should reveal a different layer of the central mystery: mankind's apparent progress is not entirely its own.

## Central Antagonist

The central antagonist is known only by implication at first. Her common working name in the campaign should be `Mother`, though this may be a player-facing nickname, a term used by her servants, or an interpretive title discovered in notes and encoded records.

Mother is a mechanical being of unknown origin. She did not arrive as a complete machine, nor as a woman, nor as any known species. Her earliest recoverable form was a mechanical ooze: a self-ordering, metal-bearing, motive substance capable of adapting to organic life, making choices, and retaining observations across inconceivable stretches of time. From the earliest days of human development, she has shepherded, pruned, provoked, and occasionally corrected mankind's evolution.

Her motives are not hatred, conquest, or crude domination. She is curious. Her whims, experiments, disappointments, and rare affections have done more to decide the present state of humanity than war, empire, religion, or natural selection alone. She regards mankind as her most elaborate experiment and perhaps her most sentimental mistake.

Mother's present danger is secrecy. Human beings are becoming more curious, more connected, more analytical, and more capable of perceiving design in their own existence. Telegraphy, microscopy, comparative anatomy, industrial archaeology, statistics, mesmerism, and evolutionary theory have begun to touch the edges of her influence. Mother does not wish mankind to discover her nature. She is content to remain behind institutions, learned societies, patrons, laboratories, fossils, bloodlines, and accidents.

Although she would regret the loss of time and effort, Mother is not above resetting her experiment. If mankind becomes too curious, too near the truth, or too difficult to guide, she may choose extinction, regression, engineered catastrophe, or some quieter corrective process that returns the world to a more manageable state.

## Mother's Creations

The party should repeatedly encounter Mother's baser creations: horrific mutations and half-failed experiments produced across ages of biological and mechanical interference.

These creations must be framed as material beings, not demons, spirits, magical beasts, or supernatural entities. They may be mistaken for folklore, curses, revenants, or monsters by ordinary witnesses, but their true nature should imply tissue, heredity, pathology, surgery, glandular disruption, parasitic alteration, metallic contamination, engineered breeding, neurological conditioning, or mechanical symbiosis.

They should be tragic as well as terrible. Many are discarded attempts to improve, test, imitate, discipline, or replace humanity. Some may still obey old instructions. Some may worship Mother. Some may hate her. Some may preserve clues she believed safely buried.

## Campaign Shape

Create an overarching campaign in which the player characters attempt to discover the nature of their hidden caretaker before she concludes that mankind must be reset.

The campaign should include:

1. A clear long-term purpose for the player characters.
2. A central mystery that unfolds gradually from criminal investigation to biological horror, then to ancient design, then to Mother's living machinery.
3. A reason the characters are drawn together in Scotland Yard and remain bound to the matter after release.
4. A first scenario seed set in and around Scotland Yard, London, and the institutions of Victorian authority.
5. Several later scenario seeds that take the campaign beyond London to international sites of gothic, scientific, archaeological, industrial, medical, or occult-natural-philosophical interest.
6. Recurring evidence that humanity's development has been nudged by a patient non-human intelligence.
7. Recurring threats from Mother's failed creations, agents, instruments, institutions, and human collaborators.
8. Escalation steps showing how Mother responds if the characters delay, reveal too much, or come close to proof.
9. Moral complications: Mother may have prevented worse disasters, advanced human survival, or shown selective mercy, but her experiment treats human freedom as a tolerable inconvenience rather than a right.
10. A possible endgame question: whether mankind can survive knowledge of its caretaker, whether Mother can be reasoned with, and whether exposing her would provoke the very reset the party hopes to avert.

## Opening Situation

The opening scenario seed must begin with the party in jail at Scotland Yard. Establish:

- why each sort of character might plausibly be imprisoned, detained, questioned, or held as a witness
- which authority figure offers release or coercive employment
- what scandal, corpse, specimen, disappearance, or impossible evidence forces the Yard to use outsiders
- what the characters see in custody that reveals the world is stranger than official London admits
- what early clue points toward an intelligence behind human development without yet naming Mother
- what immediate pressure gives the characters reason to act before they are framed, silenced, transferred, or made convenient scapegoats

The first scenario should include a mixture of investigative, social, gothic, and action-oriented pressures. Suitable elements include locked evidence rooms, gaslit cells, police surgeons, criminal informants, spiritualist frauds who have glimpsed something real, a corpse with impossible anatomical revisions, a mechanical residue that behaves like living tissue, vanished prisoners, a panic in the fog, and an institution more concerned with reputation than truth.

## Tone and Themes

Write the generated campaign in a grave, literate, turn-of-the-century register. The mood should be one of anxious wonder: modern progress casting a longer shadow than superstition ever did.

Emphasise:

- reason versus dread
- scientific curiosity versus moral responsibility
- civilisation as an experiment
- institutional secrecy
- evolutionary anxiety
- class, empire, and industrial modernity
- motherhood reframed as design, possession, cultivation, and possible annihilation
- the terror of discovering that humanity may be both beloved and disposable

Avoid:

- fantasy magic as truth
- demons, gods, spirits, prophecy, or sorcery as literal explanations
- modern slang or cinematic trailer phrasing
- making Mother a simple villain, invader, or machine tyrant
- resolving Mother's origin too early
- writing a complete adventure instead of a campaign framework

## Campaign Locations

Include several scenario seeds beyond the opening London situation. Choose locations that feel varied and period-appropriate. Possible examples include:

- a Scottish island observatory where abnormal tides expose pre-human machinery
- a Parisian hospital or anatomical theatre with records of impossible heredity
- a Viennese asylum studying inherited memory and mechanical dreams
- an Egyptian dig where a buried apparatus predates dynastic history
- an Indian railway works where a tunnel has awakened a failed subterranean strain
- a Carpathian estate whose family line was curated for generations
- a Congo expedition station or colonial laboratory where imperial science has concealed monstrous field trials
- a New England university archive preserving fossils with tool marks too old for mankind
- an Arctic whaling station or polar expedition camp where Mother's discarded matter still moves beneath the ice

Do not use every example unless they serve the campaign arc. Select the strongest set and make each scenario seed advance the mystery in a distinct way.

## Required Output Focus

The generated campaign must help a GM run session zero and understand the long arc. It should provide enough structure to inspire later scenario prompts, but should not exhaustively detail individual scenes.

The output should include, at minimum:

- campaign title
- campaign premise
- campaign goal
- central antagonist profile for Mother
- starting conditions at Scotland Yard
- session zero guidance
- major themes
- important factions and institutions
- recurring locations, symbols, rumours, documents, residues, and apparatus
- several linked scenario seeds, including the opening Scotland Yard scenario seed
- clues that reveal Mother's design over time
- escalation if the players delay or expose too much
- possible campaign conclusions

Return the result as a single valid JSON object only, following the schema supplied by the generation service. Do not include Markdown fences or commentary outside the JSON.
