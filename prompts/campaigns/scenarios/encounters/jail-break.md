# Encounter Prompt: Jail Break

Use this prompt together with the encounter generation prep prompt. Generate an encounter called `Jail Break` for the `Eyes Wide Open` scenario in the `The Enemy Within` campaign for the Turn of the Century game system.

This prompt is for encounter generation only. Do not generate a complete scenario, full campaign material, finished stat blocks, exhaustive room keys, or a scripted solution. The output should create a focused first encounter that a GM can run immediately and that can later support map, pawn, hazard, and follow-on encounter generation.

## Campaign and Scenario Context

`Jail Break` is the first encounter of both the `Eyes Wide Open` scenario and the larger `The Enemy Within` campaign. It introduces the player characters to the campaign's central dread: a hidden intelligence known later as Mother has noticed them and is capable of manipulating the very machinery, stone, gaslight, and institutional order around them.

The campaign concerns Mother, a mechanical being of unknown origin whose earliest recoverable form was a self-ordering, metal-bearing, motive ooze. She has shaped human development across immense stretches of time through curiosity, correction, and experiment. She must not be explained in this encounter. Her presence should be heard and felt, not understood.

`Eyes Wide Open` begins in Scotland Yard, London, in 1890. The player characters are in custody. They may be accused criminals, inconvenient witnesses, disgraced professionals, occult investigators, journalists, servants, constables, soldiers, debtors, medical subjects, political nuisances, or persons arrested through a mistake that was not entirely accidental.

This first encounter should establish:

- the party's immediate need to escape or secure release
- Scotland Yard as an institution of stone, iron, procedure, fear, and reputation
- Mother as an unseen intelligence whose curiosity is intimate and dangerous
- the campaign's material gothic mode: no true ghosts, spells, demons, prophecy, or fantasy magic
- the sense that the player characters have been selected for reasons they do not yet understand

## Encounter Premise

The encounter begins with the party locked together in a dark, dank, disused cell somewhere within Scotland Yard. This should feel like a forgotten holding chamber or obsolete cell block retained beneath or behind the newer institutional machinery: wet stone, rusted hinges, old manacles, bad air, guttering gas jets, a drain whose sound is too deliberate, and distant movement behind walls that no ordinary cell should possess.

A guard arrives and confronts the prisoners. He demands particulars: names, professions, alleged offences, associations, recent whereabouts, and why each of them believes they have been placed in custody. This is an intentional opportunity for player characters to disclose as much or as little of their backstories as they wish. The guard should be confrontational, impatient, class-conscious, and suspicious, but not a simple caricature. He may be frightened, poorly briefed, bribed, overworked, or obeying orders he does not understand.

After this visit, the guard leaves. The party then becomes witness to one side of a conversation. A deep woman's voice echoes through the stone prison walls, iron pipes, speaking tubes, drains, ventilation cavities, or disused conduits. The speaker appears to be conversing with someone the party cannot hear. The words they hear should convince them that the speaker does not have their best interests at heart. The conversation should imply that the party is being evaluated, that their continued survival is a matter of curiosity rather than mercy, and that some unseen interlocutor may have requested a harsher correction.

The overheard conversation must end with the female voice saying exactly:

`Oh, very well.`

As soon as those words are spoken, all the lights in the prison go out. A moment later, the same voice, now clearly speaking to the party, says exactly:

`Let's have some fun.`

The party must then find their way out of a pitch-black maze of twists and turns beneath or within Scotland Yard. Ominous sounds in the distance suggest that they are not alone. These sounds should be frightening but not yet fully explained: dragging metal, wet respiration, claws or tools on stone, a childlike sob that repeats too mechanically, a gaoler's whistle from an impossible direction, something heavy moving through a drain, or the ticking of a clockwork mechanism that changes speed when the party speaks.

## Required Encounter Shape

Generate a focused encounter with three connected movements:

1. **Custody and Confession**: the dark cell, the guard's confrontation, and the opportunity for party backstory.
2. **Mother's First Amusement**: the overheard one-sided conversation, the exact line `Oh, very well.`, the extinction of the lights, and the exact line `Let's have some fun.`
3. **The Black Maze**: the party's attempt to escape through a pitch-black, shifting, or confusing prison substructure while ominous distant sounds suggest pursuit or experiment.

The encounter should support social, investigative, exploratory, technical, stealth, and survival play. It may be combat-capable, but it should not require combat. The main objective is not to defeat a monster; it is to survive first contact with Mother's attention and emerge into the next portion of the scenario with clues, fear, and momentum.

## Location and Environment

The generated encounter should make the cell block immediately playable. Include:

- cell dimensions or relative layout
- exits, thresholds, bars, locks, hinges, drains, vents, inspection slots, gas fixtures, and improvised tools
- darkness and visibility rules or guidance suitable for a pitch-black environment
- wet stone, rust, mould, cold iron, bad air, old straw, coal smoke, river damp, and sewer odours
- distant sounds and how they shift during the encounter
- signs that the prison geometry may be older, disused, unfinished, misremembered, or deliberately altered
- environmental hazards such as flooded steps, loose gratings, sharp metal, bad air, collapsing brick, locked gates, scalding pipes, or sudden gas release
- whether a map is useful, and if so, what kind of map should be generated later

Do not present the maze as supernatural. If it seems to shift, imply darkness, panic, old construction, hidden service passages, manipulated locks, rotating gates, pressure doors, sound conduction, gas exposure, or Mother's material influence through the building.

## Participants

Generate all participants needed for the encounter, including:

- the guard who questions the party
- any unseen or delayed prison staff, such as a distant gaoler, night sergeant, turnkey, clerk, or wounded constable
- Mother's disembodied voice as a presence, pressure, or environmental actor rather than a visible NPC
- at least one possible hidden or delayed threat in the maze, which may be a failed creation, altered prisoner, manipulated guard, mechanical apparatus, or environmental hazard

Participants should have motives and behaviours. Not every human participant should be hostile. Some may be frightened, misled, trapped, drugged, bribed, or desperate to keep the prison scandal quiet.

If a creature or altered person appears, ground it in biology, pathology, surgery, conditioning, mechanical contamination, or experimental influence. Do not define it as a demon, ghost, spirit, magical beast, or supernatural entity.

## Mother's Voice

Mother's voice should be deep, feminine, intelligent, and unsettlingly composed. She should sound curious rather than theatrical, amused rather than wrathful, and intimate rather than loud. She may address the party as specimens, children, accidents, corrections, variables, or delightful irritants, but she should not reveal her name, origin, full nature, or campaign plan.

The generated encounter may include a short sample of the overheard one-sided conversation, but it must not over-explain the unheard speaker. The unheard party may be an agent, apparatus, recorded logic, subordinate intelligence, altered human, or something not yet classifiable.

Required constraints:

- include the exact line `Oh, very well.`
- include the exact line `Let's have some fun.`
- do not identify Mother by name unless as GM-facing campaign context
- do not make the voice a ghost, spirit, demon, goddess, or hallucination with no material basis
- imply possible material transmission through resonant pipework, conductive residue, telegraph interference, concealed machinery, or self-ordering metallic matter
- make the party feel observed and tested

## Player Agency

The encounter must allow multiple routes out of custody. Include at least four viable approaches, such as:

- persuading, intimidating, deceiving, bribing, or emotionally reaching the guard
- using backstory details to create legal, social, professional, or criminal leverage
- picking, forcing, sabotaging, or improvising around the cell lock
- exploiting drains, vents, inspection slots, loose stones, gas fixtures, or old service passages
- waiting for or provoking staff intervention
- using scientific, medical, mechanical, or investigative skill to interpret the blackout and maze
- cooperating with, rescuing, or bargaining with a trapped staff member or prisoner
- choosing whether to follow or avoid the ominous sounds

The generated encounter should reward player backstory without forcing confessions. A character who volunteers personal history may gain leverage, suspicion, a clue, a relationship, or Mother's particular attention. A character who remains silent should still have meaningful ways to act.

## Clues and Carry-Forward Consequences

Include clues that connect this encounter to the wider `Eyes Wide Open` scenario and `The Enemy Within` campaign. They should be subtle and material, such as:

- oily metallic residue in the mortar, drain, lock, or gas fitting
- an impossible voice pattern transmitted through old speaking tubes
- a constable's notebook mentioning a prisoner or specimen moved through unofficial channels
- a key, tag, evidence label, or transfer order pointing toward the next scenario location
- scratch marks or stains from a failed creation
- a phrase from Mother's overheard conversation that later gains significance
- a physiological effect on one or more characters after hearing the voice or passing through the maze

The encounter should end with the party out of the immediate cell block, or with a clear path into the next encounter if they fail forward. Success should not require learning the truth about Mother.

## Success and Failure

Define full success, partial success, and failure.

Full success may include escaping the cell block, preserving a clue, avoiding capture, helping or recruiting a useful NPC, and emerging with evidence that something within Scotland Yard is being concealed.

Partial success may include escaping but losing gear, attracting pursuit, abandoning a witness, being marked by Mother, or leaving evidence behind.

Failure should not end the scenario. It may result in recapture, injury, separation, blackmail, being moved to another holding area, or being released under coercive terms by an official who wants the matter suppressed.

## Tone and Style

Write the generated encounter in a grave, literate, turn-of-the-century register. The encounter should feel claustrophobic, wet, iron-bound, and morally unsafe. Let dread emerge from stone corridors, gaslight failure, echoing pipes, institutional neglect, unseen machinery, and the party's first realization that the building itself may be listening.

Emphasise:

- darkness as a practical and psychological obstacle
- custody, power, and forced self-explanation
- the prison as a moral and architectural maze
- Mother's amusement as more frightening than anger
- ominous distant sounds without immediate explanation
- science mistaken for haunting
- survival through wit, cooperation, and observation

Avoid:

- literal magic, spells, curses, demons, divine prophecy, or true ghosts
- revealing Mother's origin or full motive
- turning the encounter into a boss fight
- making the maze impossible without a single correct solution
- removing player agency through unavoidable capture or scripted escape
- modern slang, cinematic trailer phrasing, or generic fantasy language
