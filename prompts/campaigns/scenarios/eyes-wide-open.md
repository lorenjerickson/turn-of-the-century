# Scenario Prompt: Eyes Wide Open

Use this prompt together with the scenario generation prep prompt. Generate a scenario called `Eyes Wide Open` for the `The Enemy Within` campaign in the Turn of the Century game system.

This prompt is for scenario generation only. Do not write fully detailed encounters, encounter mechanics, stat blocks, complete room keys, exhaustive dialogue, or final handouts. Sketch the scenario clearly enough that later prompts can generate encounters, pawns, locations, documents, and maps from it.

## Campaign Context

`Eyes Wide Open` is the first scenario in `The Enemy Within`. Each scenario in the campaign should be self-contained, but should also reinforce the campaign's larger themes: mankind as an experiment, progress as a veil over dread, institutional secrecy, and the terrible possibility that humanity has been cultivated by an intelligence it was never meant to perceive.

The larger campaign concerns `Mother`, a mechanical being of unknown origin whose earliest recoverable form was a self-ordering, metal-bearing, motive ooze. Across immense stretches of time she has shaped, provoked, and corrected human development by whim, curiosity, and occasional affection. She does not wish mankind to discover her nature. If human curiosity advances too far, she may decide to reset the experiment rather than risk exposure.

In this first scenario, Mother must not be fully explained. She should be felt as a voice, a pressure, a pattern of impossible evidence, and a danger implied by what she is willing to discard. The player characters should leave the scenario knowing that something vast, intelligent, and inhuman has noticed them, and that the official world will not protect them from it.

## Core Premise

The scenario is set in London and elsewhere in the United Kingdom in 1890. It begins with the player characters in custody at Scotland Yard. They may be accused criminals, inconvenient witnesses, political nuisances, debtors, medical subjects, disgraced scholars, occult investigators, journalists, constables, soldiers, servants, or persons arrested by mistake. Their common bond begins in a cell.

Their first order of business is to find a way out of jail. This should not require only a single solution. The scenario should support escape, bargaining, legal pressure, technical ingenuity, social leverage, evidence gathering, alliance with an official, or a dangerous bargain with someone already compromised.

Their first exposure to Mother occurs before they leave custody: a disembodied female voice echoes from the stone walls, iron pipes, locked doors, speaking tubes, drains, or unseen cavities of Scotland Yard. The voice should be intimate, curious, and faintly disappointed rather than theatrical. It may know details no human interrogator could know. It may offer help, threaten correction, ask a question, or address the party as if they are a notable deviation in an experiment.

Mother's voice must remain materially ambiguous. Do not present it as a ghost, spirit, demon, prophecy, or spell. Imply concealed machinery, resonant pipework, conductive residue, microscopic mechanical matter, telegraphic interference, or some other repeatable but poorly understood phenomenon.

## Historical and Cultural Grounding

Draw upon real historical pressures and cultural references from Britain around 1890, while keeping the scenario playable and fictional. Use these anchors as inspiration, not as a documentary checklist:

- Scotland Yard's 1890 move from Great Scotland Yard to the Victoria Embankment, with institutional upheaval, new cells, evidence rooms, river access, unfinished passages, and bureaucratic anxiety.
- The unsolved Whitechapel murders of 1888-1891 and the public terror, press sensationalism, police embarrassment, and East End poverty surrounding them.
- The aftermath of the 1889 London dock strike, New Unionism, labour unrest, and class fear along the Thames and East End.
- The Society for Psychical Research and the period's desire to study strange experience in a scholarly, empirical manner.
- The Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn, occult lodges, séance culture, Rosicrucian interests, and respectable people hiding irrational appetites beneath formal manners.
- Highgate Cemetery and the other great Victorian cemeteries as places where architecture, class, death, botany, and secrecy meet.
- The British Museum, the Natural History Museum at South Kensington, private anatomical collections, lecture halls, hospitals, rail stations, telegraph offices, newspaper rooms, docks, and workhouses.
- Contemporary anxieties over evolution, degeneration, criminal anthropology, mesmerism, bacteriology, vivisection, heredity, industrial surveillance, imperial science, and social reform.

Keep all extraordinary events grounded in advanced science, engineering, chemistry, anatomy, pathology, heredity, electricity, mesmerism, rare materials, or experimental apparatus. Ordinary witnesses may use occult language, but the scenario's true logic must remain material.

## Geographic Scope

The party's journeys in this scenario must be restricted to the United Kingdom. The primary stage is London, but the scenario may include travel by rail, coach, river, or coastal steamer to other UK locations if doing so strengthens the investigation.

Suitable UK sites include:

- Scotland Yard and the Victoria Embankment
- Whitechapel, Spitalfields, the East End, or the Thames docks
- Highgate Cemetery or another Victorian cemetery
- the British Museum or Natural History Museum
- Bedlam, a private asylum, a hospital, or an anatomical theatre
- an occult lodge, séance parlour, or learned society chamber
- a telegraph office, newspaper office, rail terminus, or underground works
- a country estate, ruin, mine, observatory, coastal cave, or ancient stone circle in England, Scotland, or Wales

Do not send the characters outside the United Kingdom in this scenario. Later scenarios may widen the campaign.

## Scenario Goal

Generate a compact scenario arc in which the player characters:

1. escape, negotiate release, or otherwise survive custody at Scotland Yard
2. discover why they were gathered or selected
3. investigate evidence that Mother or one of her failed creations has entered London through official channels
4. learn that institutional authorities are concealing facts to preserve reputation and public order
5. prevent an immediate correction, cover-up, murder, release of altered matter, or destruction of evidence
6. obtain a clue that points toward the wider campaign without revealing Mother's full nature

The scenario should end with the party free but implicated. They should have a reason to remain together, a reason to distrust official explanations, and a reason to pursue the campaign's larger mystery.

## Antagonistic Pressure

The opposing force in this scenario is not simply Mother herself. Use a layered pressure:

- Scotland Yard officials trying to control a scandal
- a human collaborator, patron, physician, detective, occultist, scientist, or civil servant who has mistaken Mother's attention for privilege
- one failed creation or altered human specimen loose in London or hidden in custody
- Mother's distant intervention through voice, residue, machinery, dreams, telegraphy, or engineered coincidence
- public panic, newspapers, class unrest, and institutional reputation

Mother should act as a remote and curious intelligence. She should test the player characters, study their choices, and remove evidence if they become too successful. She should not arrive as a final boss, and she should not explain herself.

## Required Encounter Sketches

Include at least ten encounter concepts. Do not fully design them. Each encounter sketch should include:

- a short title
- location
- primary purpose in the scenario
- involved NPCs, hazards, clues, or opposing pressures
- likely stakes or consequence
- how it supports investigation, social manoeuvring, exploration, danger, or choice

The encounter list must form a connected narrative that the players can help shape. Include a mixture of:

- jail escape or negotiation
- first contact with Mother's disembodied voice
- police interrogation or official coercion
- evidence-room investigation
- gothic exploration at a cemetery, museum, asylum, occult site, or ancient British location
- social encounter with a witness, expert, journalist, spiritualist, labour organiser, physician, or compromised official
- pursuit through fog, station crowds, tunnels, docks, rooftops, sewers, or rail yards
- technical or scientific examination of residue, machinery, tissue, sound, telegraphy, or altered anatomy
- confrontation with one failed creation or human agent
- final choice involving exposure, rescue, escape, containment, or preservation of evidence

The first three encounter sketches must be connected to Scotland Yard and custody. The final encounter sketch should resolve the immediate danger while leaving the campaign mystery alive.

## Significant NPC Guidance

Generate NPCs suitable for the scenario, including:

- the Scotland Yard authority figure who can release, exploit, or frame the party
- a police surgeon, gaoler, clerk, detective, or evidence custodian who knows more than they admit
- a witness from the East End, docks, press, occult circles, hospital, or learned society
- a human collaborator who believes Mother represents evolutionary destiny or private salvation
- a victim or altered specimen whose condition reveals the cost of Mother's experiment
- at least one NPC who may recur in later scenarios

Each NPC should have wants, secrets, misunderstandings, and possible future use. Avoid making every official corrupt; some should be frightened, decent, proud, compromised, or trapped by hierarchy.

## Success and Failure

Define full success, partial success, and failure in a way that keeps the campaign moving.

Full success may include securing freedom, saving a witness or victim, preserving key evidence, identifying a human collaborator, and learning that Mother's influence predates modern institutions.

Partial success may include escaping but losing evidence, saving lives but being blamed, discovering a clue but alerting Mother, or exposing a scandal that drives the truth deeper underground.

Failure should not end the campaign. It may result in transfer to prison, blackmail by Scotland Yard, death or disappearance of a witness, destruction of evidence, public panic, or Mother's decision to watch the party more closely.

## Themes and Tone

Write the generated scenario in a grave, literate, turn-of-the-century register. The scenario should feel like a fogged pane through which the players glimpse the machinery behind civilisation.

Emphasise:

- custody, surveillance, and social power
- the terror of being selected
- science mistaken for spiritual visitation
- stone, iron, gaslight, river fog, telegraph chatter, and institutional silence
- the aftermath of public murders and labour unrest
- respectable occultism as both fraud and accidental doorway to truth
- human beings as evidence in an experiment
- the dread of hearing a motherly voice from a place no living throat could occupy

Avoid:

- literal magic, spells, demons, divine prophecy, or true ghosts
- resolving Mother's origin or motives completely
- travel outside the United Kingdom
- a single mandatory path out of jail
- treating the scenario as a complete campaign
- detailed encounter mechanics or stat blocks
- modern slang, cinematic trailer language, or generic fantasy phrasing

## Output Requirements

Return a scenario seed that helps a GM run a short arc of one to six sessions and later generate detailed encounters.

The output should include, at minimum:

- scenario title
- campaign connection
- scenario premise
- scenario goal
- starting conditions at Scotland Yard
- Mother's first manifestation
- historical and cultural anchors used
- primary and secondary locations
- significant NPCs
- antagonistic pressure
- success, partial success, and failure conditions
- at least ten connected encounter sketches
- three or more viable approaches to success
- pacing for one to six sessions
- clues that point toward the larger campaign
- consequences that lead into the next scenario

Return the result as a single valid JSON object only, following the schema supplied by the generation service. Do not include Markdown fences or commentary outside the JSON.
